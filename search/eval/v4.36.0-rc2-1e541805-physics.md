### How the weights were chosen

Five rounds on the dev splits, about 60 weightings in all, varying `rrf_k`,
`lexical`, `dense`, `structural`, `structural_identifiers`, `concl`, `name` and
`pagerank`. `name` and `structural_identifiers` moved nothing on these sets.

- **The conclusion lane is off (`concl: 0`).** It lowered premise MRR at every weight
  (0.280 off, 0.251 at 1.0), and it added nothing even on an ad hoc set made for it:
  the 327 dev premises whose conclusion shares its goal's head and two constants
  (fused R@10 0.280 off, 0.283 at 1.0; MRR 0.245 off, 0.222 at 1.0). Alone it reaches
  R@10 0.093 there, against 0.173 for the structural lane and 0.260 for the dense
  one: a bag of a conclusion's constants doesn't single out the lemmas that close a
  goal, and a goal's text names few of them (`ρ.fidelity σ` doesn't say
  `MState.fidelity`). Given the goal's conclusion constants as `consts`, the lane is
  neutral (MRR 0.257 off, 0.262 at 1.0); most of what `consts` gives comes through
  the structural lane. A lane weighted 0 isn't queried.
- **MathlibQR guards R@5 and MRR as well as R@10.** The best mean dev R@10 (lexical
  0.25, `rrf_k` 20, pagerank 0.016: 0.610 against 0.597) cost MathlibQR 0.046 of R@5
  and 0.031 of MRR on its test split. R@5 is what a default search shows, so a
  weighting could lose at most 0.005 of MathlibQR's dev R@5, R@10 or MRR; among
  those, dev R@10 within noise of the best, then MRR, decided.
